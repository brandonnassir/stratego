## The question

> On a new independent 4,096-pair random-opponent confirmation bank frozen
> before either arm runs, does the G1 candidate's paired 95% lower bound
> relative to the accepted Phase 8 checkpoint exceed the original `-0.01` EWR
> margin?

`H0`: Delta ≤ −0.010. `H1`: Delta > −0.010. The rule reads the lower endpoint
of the two-sided 95% paired percentile bootstrap (10,000 replicates, frozen
seed) and passes only on *strictly greater*. All of it — bank, schedule, seeds,
arm order, power basis — was committed at `9392c6ec` before a single game.

## Separating the readings

**Observed fact.** A complete, exactly-reconciled confirmation whose lower
bound clears the frozen margin with room. Both decision dialects — the
instruction's strict `>` and the original contract's `>=` — agree here; a test
pins that they can differ only at exact equality, which dyadic game scores
cannot produce at this n.

**Supported inference.** The G1 reproduction is non-inferior to the accepted
Phase 8 checkpoint against the random opponent under the original margin.
P18-D002's single failing margin was a measurement-design deficit and is
repaired by sample size alone. With P18-D002's 42/42 original gates and 7/8
certified margins already standing, **Gate G1 closes**.

**Plausible but untested.** The two banks' deltas (−0.000244 and +0.006348)
differ by about one confirmation standard error; per-bank variation of a few
thousandths is the natural reading, but two banks do not measure that
distribution. The candidate's higher flag-capture share (89.7% vs 86.7%) and
fewer draws suggest a marginally more decisive policy against random play.

**Not supported.** That the reproduction is *superior* to the accepted
checkpoint. The interval lies above zero on this bank, but superiority was not
the frozen question, no superiority margin was predeclared, and the original
bank's near-zero delta sits against it. The gate takes only what it asked for.
