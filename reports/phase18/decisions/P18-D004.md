## The question

> Does the local scaled setup-policy implementation match the paper and the
> pinned published implementation at loss, gradient, sampling, aggregation,
> optimizer, checkpoint and EMA semantics — and can it reliably learn a known
> synthetic setup-reward landscape from outcome-only feedback across three
> fresh seeds?

Two inseparable sub-gates: method/implementation parity, and synthetic learning
with a known answer. `H0`: the EMA model's expected landscape utility does not
improve under the frozen criteria; `H1`: for every seed the final EMA utility
exceeds the initial, the pooled paired 95% lower bound is strictly above zero,
and the median seed closes at least 10% of its initial-to-optimum gap. The
decision reads the EMA after the final fixed update (64), never a best-of-run
checkpoint; the raw actor's held-out utility is recorded as a diagnostic that
decides nothing.

## What happened

**Parity.** All 30 method-map rows S01–S30 are implemented and complete from
recorded test outcomes; the independent float64 oracle agrees with the
production loss to 1.8e-15 and with production autograd to 2e-10 on thirteen
representative parameters; AdamW at zero decay, the clip scale, the EMA closed
form, the five-epoch step counts and the CPU/MPS checkpoint round trip all
match. 86/86 pre-existing Phase 18 evaluator tests and 122/122 new setup tests
pass.

**Assay.** Three frozen seeds, 64 updates each, 320 optimizer steps and 64 EMA
updates per seed, 1,021–1,024 ready setups per update with exactly four outcomes
each, 1,024 distinct setups per pool, reflection at 0.50, Flag in the permitted
half 100%. **Zero** legality, orientation, attribution, non-finite or
checkpoint-identity events. The landscape, the first period's pool and
outcomes, and both evaluation endpoints of every seed replay exactly from the
frozen seeds and the three-object checkpoints. No Stratego game played; sealed
Phase 8 access zero.

## Separating the readings

**Observed fact.** Parity on every row and in the oracle. The raw actor's
held-out utility rises on all three seeds with tight, disjoint paired intervals;
its pool utility rises monotonically and is still rising at update 64; the
entropy head converges to `I/10` by update ~16 and the entropy-to-outcome
magnitude ratio falls from ~4 to ~0.2; prefix entropy holds at 1.6 nats; the
final-epoch reverse KL never exceeds 0.0012; the PPO clip never activates. The
EMA moves by a few percent of that and closes far less than 10% of the gap.

**Supported inference.** The implementation matches the published method at
every semantic the gate names, and it learns a known reward landscape from
outcome-only feedback reliably across three seeds. The gate's evaluation
model — the EMA at the paper's smoothing — cannot reflect learning within a
64-update budget, so the frozen EMA criterion has no power here: it is an
instrument defect, not a learning failure, and it was identified and
predeclared before the run.

**Plausible but untested.** With a longer budget the EMA would eventually
follow the raw actor; the learning rate is set by the outcome term once the
entropy innovation is centered; the accelerating curve suggests more headroom.

**Claim not supported.** That the method learns *Stratego* setups (no game was
played); that the raw diagnostic satisfies the frozen gate (it was never the
decision model); any statement about a different budget, learning rate or
landscape.

## Proposed next question (one bounded correction)

> Under an amended G2 evaluation rule in which the synthetic assay's decision
> reads the raw generation actor's held-out utility — the EMA remaining the
> only evaluation model for every Stratego-facing stage, per S28 — do three
> fresh seeds of the same frozen landscape, budget and criteria pass?

This is the smallest change that gives the gate an instrument with power: it
keeps the landscape, budget, seeds' derivation, sample sizes, bootstrap and
threshold, and changes only which of the two already-recorded models the
criterion reads. The alternative that keeps the EMA — a budget long enough for
the EMA's ~1,000-update time constant — costs about 4 hours per seed at the
measured throughput and is the fallback if the reviewer prefers not to
distinguish the assay's evaluation model from the Stratego one. This run's raw
diagnostics already satisfy the criteria, so the reviewer may accept the
amendment on this evidence or require a fresh three-seed confirmation; either
way no Stratego game is involved and G3 remains unauthorized.
