## The question

> Using the parity-correct setup-learning method, does the raw generation
> actor reliably learn an independently generated synthetic setup landscape
> within 64 setup updates?

The raw actor is the primary endpoint of this synthetic trainability assay
only; the EMA remains the required evaluation/deployment model for every later
Stratego-facing stage (S28) and is recorded here as telemetry that cannot
change the decision. `H0`: for at least one seed the final raw utility does
not exceed the initial, or the pooled paired 95% lower bound is at or below
zero, or the median seed closes less than 10% of its gap. `H1`: every seed
improves, the pooled lower bound is strictly above zero, and the median seed
closes at least 10%. The decision reads the raw actor after the final fixed
update (64), never a best-of-run checkpoint.

## Separating the readings

**Observed fact.** The method files, design and configuration are the G2
ones. The raw actor's held-out utility rises on every seed with disjoint,
tight paired intervals; the raw curves are still rising at update 64. The
median gap closure is 10.007% against a 10% threshold: seeds 1 and 2 clear it
individually, seed 3 (9.599%) does not, and the frozen criterion is the
median. The EMA moves by 1–2% of the raw displacement. Everything replays
bitwise.

**Supported inference.** Using the parity-correct setup-learning method, the
raw generation actor reliably learns an independently generated synthetic
setup landscape within 64 updates from outcome-only feedback. The G2
raw-actor finding replicates on a fresh table and fresh seeds, and the frozen
rule returns `PROCEED` for the synthetic trainability portion of G2. The EMA
telemetry repeats the G2 pattern (0.999^64 = 0.937975 retained, an
approximately 1,000-update time constant, severe lag within this budget); it
is recorded, not decided upon.

**Plausible but untested.** The gap-closure fraction is landscape-dependent:
in 64 updates the raw actor gained about half the utility it gained on the G2
table (+5.43 versus +10.77 utility units; 0.91 versus 1.75 uniform SDs), from
a higher initial utility toward a lower optimum, which is why the fraction sits
at the threshold's edge here and well above it in G2 (20.9%, 18.5%, 14.8%).
A fourth table from the same family could fall on either side of 10% under the
same strength of learning. A longer budget would very likely close more of the
gap and eventually carry the EMA.

**Claim not supported.** That the method learns *Stratego* setups. That the
EMA at this budget is a usable evaluation model. That the 10% threshold is
cleared robustly: the median exceeds it by 0.0073 percentage points of the
gap (about 0.004 utility units on the median seed). Any authorization of G3,
a Stratego game experiment, or Phase 8 warmstart training.

## What the reviewer should weigh

The learning signal is not in doubt: the pooled paired lower bound is 0.89
uniform SDs above zero on 12,288 paired samples, and every seed improves with
an interval whose lower end exceeds +4.9 utility units. The practical-margin
criterion is met as frozen, but at its edge, because the criterion is a
fraction of a landscape-dependent gap. That property was frozen before the
run; the rule is applied as written, and this packet does not reclassify the
margin as an instrument problem. It does recommend that the next gate's
margin be sized in the units the gate is scored in, from a known instrument
resolution, so that a pass or a fail is not decided by the draw of the table.
